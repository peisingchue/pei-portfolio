✦ About Me

Behind the Products

I grew up in Penang — Malaysia's food paradise, so yes, I'm spoilt with good food. My first career was in tourism: more than five years at Amazing Borneo in Sabah, moving from sales to marketing to running projects.

In 2022 I moved into tech and crossed the causeway to Singapore. Product management used the same muscles — understanding what people need, getting different teams on the same page, and following through until it works.

✦ Case Study  ·  0→1 Product

Loyalty Platform, Built From Zero

Loyalty & membership  ·  Live since Apr 2024

Context
Merchants wanted to own their customer relationships instead of renting them through third-party loyalty tools.
Challenge
Build a loyalty product from nothing, tightly connected to the point-of-sale, with no product playbook to follow.
My role
Sole PM, from first spec through launch and beyond.
My approach
Started from what merchants and outlet staff need day to day, defined a first version that could launch, then improved it in small steps on live merchant feedback.
Outcome
Launched in April 2024 and kept improving through 2025. The biggest lesson came after launch: support, migrations and edge cases are where trust is built.

◆ Integrated with point-of-sale
◆ Merchant-owned customer data

✦ Case Study  ·  Cross-functional

Consumer App for a Restaurant Group

Mobile app  ·  Apr – Sep 2025

Context
A restaurant group wanted its own consumer app, built on our platform.
Challenge
No product owner on the client side, several layers of stakeholders, and a developer who needed clear answers to keep moving.
My role
Owned the product decisions end to end, through to the App Store and Play Store release.
My approach
Turned scattered requests into structured documents and short presentations, so decisions actually got made. Mapped every flow to prototype detail before development, so most questions were answered up front.
Outcome
The developer had almost no questions during the build, and the app shipped on both stores. Lesson: when nobody owns the decisions, writing them down is how you own them.

✦ Product Thinking in Practice

Small calls that mattered

Matching system behaviour to how users think

Promotions that end the way merchants expect

The system and merchants had different ideas of what “ending” a campaign meant, so I made the choice explicit instead of assuming.

Cross-channel consistency

The same price, whichever way you order

Kept payment behaviour consistent across self-service channels by deciding which system owns each rule.

Balancing business goals with user friction

Helpful nudges over hard blocks

Let new members skip a sign-up step and told them what they'd miss, rather than forcing it and losing sign-ups.

Data judgement

Reports that are honest about gaps

Where old data couldn't be recovered, I labelled it clearly instead of filling the gaps with guesses.
